# 2026-06-11 — Intro call
Forty-five minutes on pricing, video, her call — prep written the night before, notes taken live, conclusions added two days later. One meeting, one document.

**Who:** Marguerite Vale (hers), Dan. **Channel:** video. **Filed here** because she called it.

## Prepared — 2026-06-10

Three questions, in the order I want them answered if we run out of time:

1. Why does the middle tier convert worse than the tier above it? My guess is the feature split, but I have no evidence.
2. Is a usage-metered component worth the billing complexity at forty customers, or only at four hundred?
3. What does she need from me to answer either of those properly — which numbers, in what form?

Deliberately not raising: the fundraise, and anything about a standing advisory arrangement. One hour, one problem.

## Notes — during

- She did not accept the framing of Q1. Her read: the middle tier is not converting worse, it is being **skipped** — buyers self-select to the top tier when the middle one is described in feature counts rather than in outcomes. "Your middle tier reads like a smaller version of the big one. Nobody wants a smaller version."
- On metering: **not at forty customers.** Her rule of thumb was that metered billing pays for itself when support load from billing questions is already material, and it is not. Revisit at ~200.
- What she wants: twelve months of tier-level revenue **and** the self-serve funnel counts at each step, in one sheet. Not a dashboard.
- Unprompted, she offered to look at the tier copy herself. That is more than the hour.
- Ran 45 minutes against a 30-minute slot; she extended it, I did not ask.

## After — 2026-06-13

**The reframe is the whole value of the call and I would not have gotten there.** I came in with a conversion problem and left with a positioning problem; every experiment I had queued was measuring the wrong thing.

Two things follow. The sheet she asked for is a half-day of work and blocks everything else, so it goes first. And her offer on the tier copy should be taken up **explicitly** rather than assumed — an offer made in the warmth of a good call is not a commitment, and treating it as one is how advisors get quietly expensive.

Metering is closed for the year. Recorded so it does not get re-litigated at the next planning session.
